01 • Project Overview

Project Overview

VITALIS is our student-developed prototype for the NASA HUNCH SFT 2026–27 Lunar Sleep-Shift Scheduling Tool. NASA HUNCH challenged us to develop an onboard, offline system that can help astronauts manage sleep and work-shift changes while reducing fatigue during missions. The concept is designed to provide personalized sleep and wake recommendations, naps, caffeine and sleep-aid timing, fatigue monitoring, and feedback while still allowing mission operations to override recommendations when necessary.

Our team turned that challenge into VITALIS, a software-based prototype focused on making the system practical and accessible on mission computers or tablets. We researched astronaut sleep, circadian rhythms, fatigue, nutrition, caffeine, and wearable technology, then moved from our early hardware/wearable concept toward a software system. We built the prototype with Flutter and Dart and developed features including crew profiles, authentication, sleep planning, fatigue scoring, shift scheduling, mission events, food and caffeine tracking, check-ins, saved recommendations, feedback, and history. We continue to test the system with different inputs and refine the scheduling logic so that recommendations can better respond to each crew member and the demands of mission operations.

02 • The Problem

Why VITALIS?

Changing schedules

Different shifts can make it difficult to keep a consistent sleep schedule.

Fatigue

Not getting enough sleep can affect alertness, concentration, memory, mood, and performance.

Mission operations

Sleep opportunities need to work around important mission events and crew responsibilities.

03 • Our Solution

How VITALIS works

Sleep Planner

Creates recommendations using sleep history, fatigue, hours awake, caffeine, shifts, and mission events.

Fatigue Score

Uses a 0–100 score to estimate fatigue and guide recommendations.

Shift Scheduling

Supports Night, Morning, and Evening shifts.

Check-In

Crew can record sleep, alertness, fatigue, and recommendation effectiveness.

Food & Caffeine

Tracks information that can be considered by the sleep planner.

Feedback

Saved feedback can help VITALIS adjust future recommendations.

05 • Prototype

What we built

VITALIS currently has nine main tabs. The information available depends on whether someone signs in as a crew member or as a crew manager.

Crew Member Account

A crew member can use the app for their own information and cannot access the full crew-management view.

  • Home: Personal status, fatigue information, and recommendations.
  • Schedule: View the schedule and mission events relevant to planning.
  • Sleep Plan: Generate and save a personalized sleep plan.
  • Crew: Opens a personal profile view rather than the full crew manager.
  • Food: Track personal food and caffeine information.
  • Shift: View the assigned work shift.
  • Check-In: Record sleep, alertness, fatigue, comments, and how effective the previous recommendation was.
  • History: Review personal check-ins, feedback, and saved sleep recommendations.
  • Settings: Change app appearance, font options, and account settings.

Crew Manager Account

A manager has access to broader crew information and can select crew members in supported screens.

  • Home: View the overall crew dashboard.
  • Schedule: Review mission events that may affect sleep opportunities.
  • Sleep Plan: Select a crew member and generate their plan using available sleep and fatigue information.
  • Crew: View and manage crew profiles.
  • Food: Select crew members and review their food and caffeine tracking.
  • Shift: Review and manage crew shift assignments.
  • Check-In: Select a crew member and save a check-in and recommendation feedback.
  • History: Review crew members’ check-ins, feedback, and saved recommendations.
  • Settings: Change app appearance, font options, and account settings.

Authentication

Email sign-in and verification identify the account and its assigned role.

Local Data

The prototype stores operational app data in a local SQLite database, including sleep logs, mission events, shifts, food records, feedback, and recommendations.

Recommendation Feedback

Check-ins can be linked to the recommendation being rated, allowing the system to consider past effectiveness when planning.

The role-based screens are part of the current prototype. Full secure multi-device data synchronization and public deployment are future work, not completed features.

09 • Testing

How we tested and what we learned

Testing has been used to find weaknesses in the recommendations, check that new features save information correctly, and guide the next round of improvements. The results described here are prototype-development findings, not clinical validation.

1. Initial test

We began by testing the early prototype with five test subjects. We compared the sleep recommendations it produced and checked whether the results seemed individualized.

2. Main issue found

The first version recommended the same 22:00 sleep time to everyone. This showed that the prototype was not responding enough to differences between users.

3. Changes made

We expanded the sleep planner so it could consider more information instead of relying on one general sleep time.

Inputs we now check

  • Recent sleep history and estimated sleep debt
  • Hours the person has been awake
  • Previous fatigue score
  • Caffeine recorded for the day
  • Assigned work shift
  • Mission events and possible sleep conflicts
  • Check-in alertness rating
  • How effective the previous recommendation was

App and data checks

  • Generated sleep plans and saved recommendations
  • Check-ins recording sleep and feedback
  • Feedback linked to the recommendation being rated
  • History displaying check-ins and saved recommendations
  • Different views for crew members and managers
  • Database version update and retention of existing data during migration
  • Flutter Analyzer to check the code for reported issues

What testing has shown so far

The first test revealed that a fixed sleep time was not enough. As the prototype developed, the planner gained more inputs related to sleep, fatigue, caffeine, shifts, mission events, and feedback. These changes make the plan more responsive to the information entered, but they do not prove that every recommendation is correct or medically validated. Some different inputs may still produce similar results, so the scheduling logic needs continued refinement.

What we still need to test

  • Combinations of short sleep, high fatigue, long hours awake, and caffeine use.
  • Mission events that overlap with a planned sleep period, including several conflicts on the same day.
  • Changes between Night, Morning, and Evening shifts.
  • Whether plans remain reasonable when little or no sleep history is available.
  • Whether repeated check-ins and effectiveness ratings consistently influence later recommendations.
  • Whether each role sees only the appropriate information when tested across more accounts and devices.
  • More test cases with different users and edge cases before making stronger claims about performance.

    Helped us understand food considerations in space and informed the context for the food-tracking feature.

  2. National Aeronautics and Space Administration. (n.d.). Risk of inadequate food and nutrition. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/risk-of-inadequate-food-and-nutrition/

    Supported our understanding of why nutrition matters for crew health and performance.

  3. National Aeronautics and Space Administration. (n.d.). Space food systems. https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/hhp/space-food-systems/

    Provided background on planning and managing food for spaceflight, informing our prototype food and inventory ideas.

  4. National Aeronautics and Space Administration. (n.d.). JSC food and nutrition. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-food-nutrition/

    Helped ground our food and nutrition research in NASA’s crew-health context.

    Provided broader context for the systems and crew needs that a spaceflight tool must consider.

    Helped us consider habitability and crew-support requirements when framing the project.

  7. National Aeronautics and Space Administration. (n.d.). Artemis II: What’s on the menu? https://www.nasa.gov/missions/artemis/artemis-2/artemis-ii-whats-on-the-menu/

    Provided an example of food planned for a real NASA mission and helped inform our NASA-inspired prototype food list.

  8. National Aeronautics and Space Administration. (n.d.). Space nutrition. https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/Space_Nutrition_Book.pdf

    Provided additional background on nutrition during spaceflight. Our in-app food values remain prototype estimates, not official NASA nutritional data.

  9. Flutter. (n.d.). Flutter documentation. https://docs.flutter.dev/

    Used as a reference while building the app interface, navigation, screens, and desktop prototype.

  10. Dart. (n.d.). Dart documentation. https://dart.dev/

    Supported our coding work and helped us understand the language used to build VITALIS.

  11. Supabase. (n.d.). Supabase documentation. https://supabase.com/docs

    Used while implementing email authentication and user profiles for account roles.

  12. FlutterBluePlus contributors. (n.d.). FlutterBluePlus. pub.dev. https://pub.dev/packages/flutter_blue_plus

    Helped us research possible Bluetooth connectivity for future wearable or sensor integration.

  13. sqflite_common_ffi contributors. (n.d.). sqflite_common_ffi. pub.dev. https://pub.dev/packages/sqflite_common_ffi

    Supported our local SQLite database setup for storing prototype information on a desktop computer.
